# Failed clinical trials, data access and development pivots
Evidence review as of 8 September 2026. Window: 8 September 2016–8 September 2026.

## Scope and interpretation
This is a curated, cross-therapeutic-area review of high-profile failures, not an exhaustive census of every failed clinical trial worldwide. It contains 34 trial-program entries representing 45 named trials. Trials are included for failure of a primary efficacy test, interim futility, or an unfavorable benefit–risk decision. Funding-only discontinuations, regulatory refusals without a trial failure, and manufacturing-only failures are excluded.

Selection emphasizes consequential phase III programs and selected phase II programs with useful reanalysis lessons. First public failure dates determine inclusion. Later publications and development outcomes are used to explain what happened. A drug's failure in one indication does not imply failure in every indication. “Pharma companies” is assumed to be the intended meaning of “pharmacists”; pharmacist roles are addressed separately below.

Aducanumab is a special case: both trials were initially stopped for futility, but fuller EMERGE data later met the primary endpoint, while ENGAGE remained negative. Thus 45 is a count of named trials implicated in these negative or stopped programs, not 45 permanently negative final primary analyses. CREAD2 and REFOCUS were stopped following other trial readouts and are not equivalent to fully completed independent failures.

## What does “data available” mean?
- Public disclosure: an announcement, paper, presentation, or registry results summary. It may contain only topline information and is not necessarily a reusable dataset.
- R: a trial-specific patient-level data request route or sharing statement was located. Access remains conditional on review, agreements, permitted purposes, and the variables released. No request was submitted and access was not tested.
- D: public derived research data/code identified, without verification that this is the full original participant-level dataset.
- U: patient-level access was not verified in this review. It does NOT mean the data do not exist or cannot be requested.

For independent drug-response reanalysis, request the original protocol and amendments, statistical analysis plan, randomization/treatment assignments, longitudinal outcomes, discontinuation and missingness reasons, baseline covariates, drug exposure/adherence, adverse events, data dictionary and relevant biomarkers. Placebo-only data can support progression modeling but cannot by themselves establish a drug-treatment interaction. A publication's aggregate tables cannot reliably recover these relationships.

## How analysis after failure changed development
Post hoc means an analysis not specified before seeing the relevant outcomes. A prespecified subgroup, prespecified secondary endpoint, later follow-up, and post hoc analysis are different evidence types. All are distinguished here.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anifrolumab; AstraZeneca | TULIP-1 missed SRI-4, a composite lupus response measure. | Evaluation showed a more favorable response using BICLA, an alternative measure already collected in the program. | AstraZeneca chose BICLA for TULIP-2 after evaluating TULIP-1. | TULIP-2 succeeded; Saphnelo received US approval in 2021. ## Problem statement
Biopharma teams deciding what to do after a negative trial must distinguish lack of biological activity from inappropriate exposure, patient heterogeneity, an insensitive outcome measure, inadequate follow-up, background-treatment effects, or operational problems. Aggregate publications rarely provide enough information to separate these explanations. Unrestricted subgroup searching, meanwhile, can generate false-positive “rescue” stories.

A useful product or specialist service would integrate authorized participant-level data, the original protocol/statistical plan and biological evidence; reproduce the primary result; identify and stress-test a small number of plausible explanations; and produce an auditable stop, redesign, retest or repurpose decision. Independent or prospective confirmation should be an explicit next step.

The defensible value proposition is better development decisions and more informative next trials. Avoid presenting guaranteed drug rescue or retrospective significance as the product.

## Market and who benefits
The inventory identifies each asset’s therapeutic market. The commercial market for a reanalysis business is different: its direct buyers are organizations with drug-development budgets and legitimate data access.

| Buyer / user | Specific problem | Useful paid deliverable | Value measure | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-size biotechs with a failed phase II/III asset | Limited runway and uncertainty over whether to restart, license or abandon | Fixed-scope independent reanalysis, dose/subgroup hypotheses, next-trial design | A justified financing/licensing decision; avoiding an uninformative repeat trial | Full data access, small sample sizes and selection bias |
| Large pharma clinical-development and translational teams | Many indications, competing investment priorities, heterogeneous evidence | Repeatable portfolio review; exposure-response and biomarker analyses; validation across trials | Better allocation of development budget; reproducible go/no-go decisions | Integration into existing statistics/governance workflows |
| CROs and specialist biostatistics providers | Need to offer sponsor-ready analysis and protocol redesign | Validated analysis workflows, simulations and reproducible reporting | Analyst productivity and winning development-services work | Sponsor usually controls data rights and the development decision |
| Asset-acquisition or licensing teams | Need to assess whether an abandoned molecule has a credible new path | Independent diligence on original failure, subgroup robustness and new indication rationale | Better transaction selection and credible milestone design | Drug IP, exclusivity, data rights and cost of prospective confirmation |
| Academic groups / disease foundations | Need to learn from trials and improve future recruitment/endpoints | Authorized secondary analyses and shared methods | External validation, publications and trial-design improvements | Research-use restrictions and grant budgets |
| Clinical pharmacists / pharmacometricians | Need to understand exposure, dose, interactions and adherence | Dose/exposure-response studies supporting sponsor decisions | More defensible dosing and interaction hypotheses | Usually expert users/collaborators, not owners of drug-development rights |

These buyer and product conclusions are analytical inferences from the cases, not a measured customer-demand survey. The reviewed sources do not establish that a particular CRO independently caused any of these drug pivots; sponsor and investigator contributions are better documented.

### Market sizing without a false TAM
No defensible global dollar estimate for “failed-trial reanalysis” was established by this review. The entire CRO market, drug-repurposing market, or therapeutic-market revenue should not be substituted for it.

A practical bottom-up annual revenue model is:
eligible paying sponsors × analyses purchased per year × realized fee per analysis.

Illustrative planning scenarios only—not observed market size, market pricing or forecasts:

## Practical starting points
1. A4: strongest directly visible research-access starting point for progression, biomarker and endpoint work; verify treatment-arm coverage for any efficacy question.
2. TULIP-1: useful endpoint-sensitivity example with later prospective confirmation and a published request route.
3. PASADENA: useful heterogeneity/motor-outcome case, with explicit limits around exploratory findings.
4. AMARANTH: strong reproducible-methods demonstration because associated code/derived data are public, but independent validation is essential.
5. PIVOT IO-001: useful negative-control case for understanding target engagement versus therapeutic benefit, rather than expecting every analysis to produce a restart.

To turn this selected review into a near-exhaustive census, define jurisdictions and trial phases, extract registries for the date window, inspect reasons for stopping, and match completed trials to protocols, results, papers and sponsor disclosures. Completed status alone does not establish success; terminated status alone does not establish scientific failure. Deduplicate by registry identifier and distinguish trial, arm and drug-indication counts. Missing disclosure creates an unavoidable completeness limitation.

## Limitations
Search-based evidence review, not a preregistered systematic review or raw-data audit. More published failures are more likely to enter the list, so public-disclosure percentages are selection-biased. Latest 2026 events may have only topline evidence. Patient-level requestability is incompletely ascertained; U is deliberately not “unavailable.” No patient data were downloaded or reanalyzed. No claim is made that subgroup findings justify clinical use.
